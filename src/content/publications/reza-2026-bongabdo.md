The essay questions the popular attribution of the Bengali calendar to the Mughal emperor Akbar or to Shashanka, the seventh-century ruler of Gauda. It proposes instead that the calendar is ‘a long-term expression of Bengal’s delta civilisation, later changed and adapted by different rulers’, holding signs of ‘monsoon ecology, life along rivers, farming traditions, Buddhist institutions, local astronomy, Shaivite state-building, and Mughal administrative changes’, each layer added ‘without wiping out the earlier ones’.

The author note describes him as ‘the originator of the new contextualism framework’ and as working on ‘Bengal’s cultural history, deltaic urbanism, heritage governance, and civilisational continuity’. The essay itself does not discuss New Contextualism.
