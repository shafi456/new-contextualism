Written as the author prepared to present the approach at the State Heritage Conference 2025 in Perth, Australia, the essay presents New Contextualism as ‘a critical framework’ rather than a prescriptive style, developed over more than a decade at BRAC University.

It sets out five tenets: forward-thinking design anchored in prediction; historical grounding as foundational knowledge; holistic context understanding through a ‘tenfold matrix’ (the most explicit published definition of the ten dimensions); balancing the universal and the particular through hybridisation; and designing for adaptability and resilience. It then describes New Contextualism 2.0, with four areas of emphasis: nature-inspired design, deep sustainability integration, alignment with the UN Sustainable Development Goals and proactive resilience.

The essay discusses heritage as a living continuum and illustrates the approach with academic proposals for Chawk Bazar in Old Dhaka, the Al-Cazaba in Spain and Alpana Gram in Chapainawabganj.
