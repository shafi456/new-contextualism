An article in *Esempi di Architettura*, the International Journal of Architecture and Engineering, in an issue edited by Olimpia Niglio and Rana P. B. Singh. According to its abstract, the paper repositions Somapura Mahavihara, a seventh-century Buddhist monastery inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985, within the discourse of sacred heritage management, ‘proposing a New Contextualist framework to restore its spiritual aura and amplify its global appeal’.

Drawing on Buddhist pilgrimage centres including Bodh Gaya, Lumbini and Bagan, it argues for ‘an integrated management strategy that harmonizes conservation of tangible and intangible heritage, community participation, and sustainable tourism innovation’, in which sacred tourism is treated as both an economic engine and a means of cultural and spiritual renewal.
