The scientific and technological dimension concerns what can be known and what can be built: material properties and technological possibilities [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. It includes the material intelligence of earlier builders (the founder describes early Buddhist monasteries in Bengal that responded to climate and used local materials such as terracotta [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]) as well as contemporary tools of analysis.

New Contextualist writing gives particular weight to prediction. Data analytics, scenario planning and expert insight are presented as ways of anticipating future scenarios [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path]; big data and AI can model scenarios and forecast stresses if they are used responsibly [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice], and their accuracy depends on an understanding of historical precedent [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh].

Published formulations name this dimension differently: ‘scientific’ in 2023 and April 2025, and ‘Scientific/Technological’ in the ten-layer lens of September 2025 [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].
