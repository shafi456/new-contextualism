The social dimension asks who a place belongs to and how it is lived. It attends to community structures, demographics and lifestyles [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]: the networks, routines and forms of collective life through which people inhabit, share and contest space.

In New Contextualist writing the social dimension is bound to equity. Design ‘must protect the “right to the city,”’ ensuring that no project strips away people’s access to space, belonging or shared resources [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. The same essay identifies social fragmentation (gentrification, high-rise isolation and the privatisation of public space) as one of the crises that contemporary practice has failed to address [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].
