The ecological layer concerns the living systems a place depends on: climate, topography, biodiversity and resources [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. In the deltaic setting from which New Contextualism emerged, it begins with water: monsoon rain, rivers and canals, and the settlement patterns they have shaped.

New Contextualist writing criticises architecture that ‘treats the environment as a backdrop rather than a lifeblood’, citing paved-over wetlands, urban heat islands and the loss of Dhaka’s river and canals to encroachment [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. Its later formulation, New Contextualism 2.0, adds nature-inspired design, a deeper integration of sustainability and an emphasis on proactive resilience to sea-level rise and storms [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh].
