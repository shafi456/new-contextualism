## Intellectual Origins


New Contextualism emerged from the intersection of architectural practice, historical research, and academic exploration under the initiative of Mohammad Habib Reza.

Its development was influenced by practical experiences in the urban environment of Barisal, where architectural and planning interventions required an understanding of the relationships between settlement patterns, water systems, local livelihoods, environmental conditions, and community structures.

These experiences demonstrated that successful development in a deltaic environment requires more than the introduction of new architectural forms. It requires an understanding of the geographical, ecological, economic, and cultural processes that have shaped the existing settlement.

A second intellectual foundation came from research into the early Buddhist architecture of Bengal, including historical sites such as Mahasthangarh and Wari-Bateshwar. This research explored how architectural form, spatial organisation, materials, climate, religious practices, and social structures were interconnected.

The study of these historical environments reinforced the importance of understanding architecture through the conditions that produced it rather than examining buildings as independent objects.

The philosophy also developed through critical engagement with international architectural discourse, including contextualism, critical regionalism, urban design, community-oriented planning, and environmental sustainability.

## Development of the Theory

The philosophy gradually evolved through academic teaching, architectural studios, research, workshops, and professional discussions at BRAC University.

Its development involved encouraging students to investigate the broader context of their design sites through multiple interconnected dimensions. This approach gradually established a ten-fold method of contextual investigation, providing a foundation for more informed architectural and urban proposals.

The public articulation of New Contextualism developed through successive publications and academic engagements. Early formulations emphasised historical understanding, future-oriented design, and contextual diversity. Later developments expanded the discussion to include interdisciplinary analysis, adaptability, social equity, environmental responsibility, and the role of emerging technologies.
