The published texts define New Contextualism in several ways: as a philosophy, as a critical framework and as a method. They agree on its core, a reading of context far wider than the physical site, held together by a concern for the future and for justice.

## What Is New Contextualism?

The founder’s first extended essay defines it in a single sentence:

> ‘New Contextualism is a forward-thinking philosophy that advocates for creating built environments informed by accurate predictions of the future, deeply rooted in the past and encompassing a holistic understanding of the context.’ [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path]

The same essay adds that the approach ‘emphasises equity and justice, promoting inclusive and diverse designs that benefit all community members’ [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path]. In 2025 the founder described it as ‘a critical framework—a way of thinking that advocates for a design process that is intrinsically forward-looking, yet profoundly anchored in an understanding of the past’, with ‘a commitment to equity and justice’ central to its ethos [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh].

The September 2025 essay puts the emphasis on method. New Contextualism is ‘a design philosophy that positions itself to ensure buildings are not isolated objects but catalysts for belonging, well-being, and resilience’, and what is new about it ‘lies not in style but in approach’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

## Why New Contextualism?

The texts present the approach as a response to the shortcomings of the paradigms that shaped the twentieth-century city. Modernism, the founder writes, ‘often arrived in contexts like ours as an external imposition’, while Postmodernism ‘swung towards irony and surface quotation’ [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. Classic contextualism ‘often stopped at the surface’, and placemaking, though it ‘brought people back into the conversation’, remained limited to small-scale interventions [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

The September 2025 essay also points to the present condition of cities: ‘multiple overlapping crises in the urban built environment’, including ecological fragility, social fragmentation and the cultural homogenisation by which ‘the “anywhere city” erases belonging’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. Its conclusion is that ‘in a world where too many buildings could be anywhere and thus belong nowhere, we need an approach that offers a way to create places rooted in culture, yet open to change’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

## Scope and Intellectual Contribution

New Contextualism is presented as a framework for architecture and urbanism alike: ‘a layered framework for architecture and urbanism integrating ecology, equity, memory, and belonging’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. It claims to operate across three ranges at once:

> ‘a replicable, layered framework capable of operating across time (past, present, future), scope (from ecology to culture), and scale (repeatable framework building to city).’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]

Its sources are interdisciplinary. The founder describes the approach as having been refined at BRAC University by ‘incorporating ideas from various disciplines, including anthropology, sociology and environmental science’ [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path]. The September 2025 essay describes it as ‘process-based rather than style-based, incorporating systems thinking, regenerative ethics, and participatory design’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

Its proponents describe its contribution in two ways at once. It is offered as ‘a holistic context-agnostic framework where design engages principles and ten intertwined layers; only the answers change with place’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice], and the founder’s 2025 biography positions it ‘as both a Global South contribution to architectural thought and a globally relevant method for resilient futures’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. How these claims have been received outside the circle of the approach’s proponents is discussed under [Critical Perspectives and Open Questions](/philosophy/critical-perspectives/).
