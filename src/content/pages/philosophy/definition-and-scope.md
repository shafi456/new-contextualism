## What Is New Contextualism?

New Contextualism is a developing philosophy of architecture and urbanism that places contextual understanding at the centre of the design process. It seeks to establish a meaningful relationship between the conditions inherited from the past, the realities of the present, and the possibilities of the future.

The philosophy considers architecture not simply as the production of buildings, but as an active process of shaping relationships between human communities and their environments. A building, public space, neighbourhood, or city is understood through the wider systems in which it exists.

Context therefore extends beyond physical surroundings. It encompasses the social, cultural, historical, political, economic, ecological, geographical, scientific, urban, and architectural conditions that collectively influence a place.

The objective is not to reproduce existing forms or preserve every existing condition unchanged. Rather, it is to interpret the underlying relationships, values, knowledge, and processes that give a place its identity, and use them to inform appropriate contemporary interventions. 

## Why New Contextualism?

Architectural and urban development has frequently been influenced by universal design models that are transferred between places without sufficient consideration of their local conditions. Although these approaches have contributed to technological advancement and new architectural possibilities, their application can sometimes produce environments disconnected from local climate, culture, livelihoods, and community needs.

Conventional contextual approaches have also tended to prioritise visible relationships such as scale, proportion, materials, and architectural character. While these considerations remain important, they do not necessarily address the deeper ecological, social, historical, and economic systems that shape a place.

New Contextualism responds to this limitation by expanding the understanding of context and making it an integral part of design thinking. It encourages architects and urban designers to move beyond visual compatibility towards a more comprehensive understanding of the forces that influence the built environment.

It also recognises contemporary challenges such as climate change, environmental degradation, social inequality, cultural homogenisation, and the fragmentation of urban communities. Addressing these challenges requires design approaches that are locally informed, interdisciplinary, inclusive, and capable of adapting to changing conditions.

## Scope and Intellectual Contribution

New Contextualism operates across different geographical and architectural scales, from individual buildings and settlements to neighbourhoods, cities, and regions.

Its scope is also temporal, connecting historical knowledge, contemporary conditions, and future scenarios. This relationship allows design to respond not only to existing needs but also to emerging environmental, social, and technological challenges.

The philosophy draws upon architecture, urbanism, environmental science, sociology, anthropology, history, economics, and related fields. It encourages the integration of diverse forms of knowledge into a coherent design process.

Its intellectual contribution lies in establishing contextual understanding as a comprehensive foundation for architectural and urban decision-making, while placing particular emphasis on sustainability, equity, cultural identity, and long-term resilience.
