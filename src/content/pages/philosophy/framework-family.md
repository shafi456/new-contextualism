The platform’s structure names a family of frameworks developed from New Contextualism for particular fields. Of these, only New Contextualism itself is defined in published texts. The name New Contextual Heritage Management appears in the subtitle of a 2026 article. The others have not yet been defined in any source the editors have found. They are listed here with what the record does say about each field, and will be defined here once the founder has published or approved a definition.

<!-- module: provenance kind="editorial-summary" approval="pending" note="Definitions awaited from the founder" -->

## New Contextualism (NC)

The foundational philosophy: ‘a forward-thinking philosophy that advocates for creating built environments informed by accurate predictions of the future, deeply rooted in the past and encompassing a holistic understanding of the context’ [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path]. Its definition, origins, principles and layers are set out in the other pages of this section, beginning with [Definition and Scope](/philosophy/definition-and-scope/).

## New Contextual Urbanism (NCU)

> [!PENDING] Not yet defined in a published source
> No published text found by the editors uses the name New Contextual Urbanism.

The published texts already extend New Contextualism to urbanism. The approach is described as ‘a layered framework for architecture and urbanism’ that operates at scales from ‘building to city’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice], and its principle of equity commits design to protect ‘the “right to the city”’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. A 2026 author note describes the founder’s work on ‘deltaic urbanism’ [@reza-2026-bongabdo]. A similar-sounding term, ‘neo-contextual resilient urbanism’, is attributed in the report of the 2023 workshop to S M Kaikobad, one of its speakers, rather than to the founder [@bracu-2023-workshop-report]. See [Urbanism and Urban Design](/methodologies/urbanism/).

## New Contextual Heritage Management (NCHM)

The name appears in the title of an article by Reza and Rahman, ‘Re-Establishing the Sacredness of Somapura Mahavihara: A New Contextual Heritage Management of a Buddhist Sacred Land’ [@reza-rahman-2026-somapura]. Its abstract proposes ‘a New Contextualist framework’ for the site and argues for ‘an integrated management strategy that harmonizes conservation of tangible and intangible heritage, community participation, and sustainable tourism innovation’ [@reza-rahman-2026-somapura]. The abstract does not set out a general definition of the framework. See [Heritage Management](/methodologies/heritage-management/).

## New Contextual Design Methodology (NCDM)

> [!PENDING] Not yet defined in a published source
> No published text found by the editors uses the name New Contextual Design Methodology.

The published texts describe a design method without giving it this name: the ‘tenfold overlapping study’ taught in the founder’s studios [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path], and the three phases with which ‘every project begins’, Read, Plan and Process [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. At the 2024 workshop Mohammad Zillur Rahman ‘elaborated on the methodology of implementing New Contextual theory into architectural and urban projects’ [@arch-bangla-2024-workshop-report]. See [Architectural Design](/methodologies/architectural-design/) and [Read–Plan–Process](/methodologies/read-plan-process/).

## Contextual Intelligence (CI)

> [!PENDING] Not yet defined in a published source
> No published text of New Contextualism found by the editors uses the term Contextual Intelligence. A definition will be added here when the founder publishes or approves one.

The nearest published wording describes settlements ‘intelligently responsive to our specific ecological and social context’ [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh], and calls for ‘intelligently reinterpreting the past to inform new directions’ [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path]. See [Contextual Intelligence](/methodologies/contextual-intelligence/).

## Related Research Theories

The founder’s research on the history of Bengal is related to New Contextualism through his biography, not through a published argument linking the two. A 2026 author note places ‘the new contextualism framework’ alongside his work on ‘Bengal’s cultural history … and civilisational continuity’ [@reza-2026-bongabdo].

- **Syncretic Cultural History Theory (SCHT).** The name has not been found in a published source. The founder’s essay of June 2026 calls for ‘what might be called a syncretic cultural history’, defined as ‘a method of reading layered civilisational formation’ [@reza-2026-reading-bengal].
- **Ganga-Brahmaputra Civilization (GBC).** The name has not been found in a published source. The same essay traces ‘a civilisational logic of its own: rooted in the Ganges-Brahmaputra delta’ [@reza-2026-reading-bengal], and an essay of May 2026 describes ‘Bengal’s delta civilisation’ [@reza-2026-bongabdo].
- **SDG+.** At the 2024 workshop, Md Samiur Rahman Bhuiyan suggested that the approach ‘could be termed SDG+ (Sustainable Development Goal Plus)’, for its alignment with the Sustainable Development Goals [@arch-bangla-2024-workshop-report].

These are set out under [Civilizational and Historical Research](/research/civilizational-history/).
