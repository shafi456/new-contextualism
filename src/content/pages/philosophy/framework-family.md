The platform’s structure names a family of frameworks developed from New Contextualism for particular fields. Of these, only New Contextualism itself is defined in published texts. The name New Contextual Heritage Management appears in the subtitle of a 2026 article. The others have not yet been defined in any source the editors have found. They are listed here with what the record does say about each field, and will be defined here once the founder has published or approved a definition.

## New Contextualism (NC)

New Contextualism is the foundational philosophy that establishes a comprehensive approach to understanding relationships between architecture, people, place, and time. It provides the conceptual basis for investigating contextual conditions and informing design decisions through historical understanding, future-oriented thinking, interdisciplinary knowledge, sustainability, and social equity. Its definition, origins, principles and layers are set out in the other pages of this section, beginning with [Definition and Scope](/philosophy/definition-and-scope/).

## New Contextual Urbanism (NCU)

New Contextual Urbanism extends the principles of New Contextualism into the planning, design, and transformation of urban environments. It considers cities as interconnected social, ecological, cultural, economic, political, and spatial systems. Its focus extends beyond individual buildings to include neighbourhoods, public spaces, infrastructure, settlement patterns, and regional relationships. It seeks to support urban environments that are inclusive, ecologically responsive, resilient, and attentive to the everyday needs of their communities. See [Urbanism and Urban Design](/methodologies/urbanism/).

## New Contextual Heritage Management (NCHM)

New Contextual Heritage Management applies contextual thinking to the conservation, interpretation, and management of heritage environments. It considers heritage as more than the preservation of physical structures. Historical landscapes, cultural practices, community relationships, collective memory, and the continuing significance of a place are also essential considerations. Its emerging application seeks to connect heritage conservation with community participation, environmental responsibility, and sustainable development. See [Heritage Management](/methodologies/heritage-management/).

## New Contextual Design Methodology (NCDM)

Contextual Intelligence refers to the capacity to recognise, interpret, and respond to the complex conditions that influence a particular place. It involves understanding relationships between contextual dimensions, identifying their relative significance, and translating research into informed decisions. As a developing concept, it offers a potential direction for exploring how contextual knowledge can support architectural judgement, interdisciplinary collaboration, and responsible design. See [Contextual Intelligence](/methodologies/contextual-intelligence/).
