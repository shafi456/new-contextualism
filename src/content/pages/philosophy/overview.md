New Contextualism is an evolving architectural and urban design philosophy developed by Mohammad Habib Reza. It proposes a comprehensive way of understanding the relationship between the built environment, people, nature, culture, and time.

Rather than approaching architecture as an isolated physical intervention, New Contextualism considers design as a response to the complex conditions that shape a particular place. These conditions extend beyond the immediate site and include social structures, cultural practices, historical memory, political systems, economic realities, ecological processes, geographical characteristics, technological developments, urban relationships, and architectural traditions.

The philosophy recognises that every place is continuously shaped by its past, experienced through its present, and transformed by its future. Consequently, architectural and urban interventions must be informed by historical understanding, present-day realities, and the ability to anticipate future conditions.

New Contextualism does not prescribe a particular architectural language or stylistic expression. Instead, it establishes a critical and interdisciplinary approach to understanding context and translating that understanding into meaningful design decisions. Its fundamental concerns include environmental responsibility, social equity, cultural continuity, resilience, and the creation of places that support human well-being.

This section explores the intellectual foundations, principles, contextual dimensions, theoretical relationships, and continuing development of New Contextualism.
