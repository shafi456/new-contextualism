## Competing Contextual Priorities

Not every layer counts equally in every project: ‘not every layer carries equal weight each time, but together they ensure a fuller picture’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. The published case studies show the weighting in practice, with one thesis led by spiritual and cultural layers and another by ‘ecology, urban, and governance’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

## Ethical and Equity Considerations

The September 2025 essay presents the framework itself as an answer to the ethics of trade-offs. When ‘trade-offs become difficult, ethics are overlooked’, it argues; ‘therefore, a structured advanced framework is necessary where choices can align around values caring for people, place, and planet that goes beyond style-chasing’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

Its principle of equity sets a limit on every choice: design must ensure ‘that no project strips away people’s access to space, belonging, or shared resources’, and architecture ‘should be inclusive, equitable, and responsive to diverse needs’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

## Scenario Testing

Anticipating the future is a principle of the approach. Design ‘must anticipate the trajectory of societal needs, technological advancements, and environmental shifts’ [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. The tools named for this are ‘data analytics, scenario planning and expert insights’ [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path], and big data and AI, which ‘provide tools to model scenarios, forecast stresses, and anticipate needs’ when used responsibly [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. New Contextualism 2.0 adds proactive resilience: designing to ‘anticipate and adapt to future climate change impacts, such as sea-level rise and increased storm intensity’ [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh].

## Monitoring and Learning

New Contextualism 2.0 proposes ‘explicitly mapping design intentions and outcomes against the global framework of the SDGs’ [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh; @united-nations-2015-agenda-2030].
