The framework rests on ‘a tenfold overlapping analysis’ of context [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh], applied across scales from the site to the world. Its layers and their terms are from the founder’s writing; the relations between them are an editorial interpretation, marked as such.

## Applying the Ten Contextual Layers

The ten layers are applied together, not in turn. The analysis ‘ensures that design interventions are not simplistic or unidimensional’, and ‘prevents the imposition of predetermined solutions and fosters locally attuned responses’ [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. In the September 2025 formulation, ‘not every layer carries equal weight each time, but together they ensure a fuller picture’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

The published case studies show how the weighting varies. One thesis foregrounded spiritual, historical, cultural and social layers; another prioritised ‘ecological, social, political, and urban layers’, demonstrating ‘how the tenfold reading adapts: one thesis may begin with spiritual and cultural roots, another with ecology, urban, and governance, yet both arrive at a design that belongs’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

## Relationships Between the Layers

The published texts describe the layers as overlapping, but they do not specify how each relates to the others. The relations recorded here are proposed by the editors as the most direct; several are illustrated by examples from the published record. Every layer relates to every other in some way; the diagram and the matrix mark only the most direct relations.

## Scales of Context

Context, in the founder’s 2023 essay, ‘starts from the space's universal values, extends to regional ideas aligned with critical regionalism, and encompasses the immediate neighbourhood and site boundaries’, each examined through its past and its developmental trends [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path]. The September 2025 essay describes context as operating ‘across scales from micro to macro, local to global’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

<!-- module: scales -->

### Site and Building

The innermost scale, within the site’s boundaries [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path]. The framework is described as repeatable from building to city [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]; at this scale the architectural layer, with its typologies, styles and precedents, is read most closely [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh].

### Neighbourhood and Urban

The immediate neighbourhood [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path] and the urban fabric of which it is part. In the Kallyanpur thesis, ‘at the neighborhood scale, a transit station was designed as the first point of interaction with the canal during daily commutes’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

### Regional

‘Regional ideas aligned with critical regionalism’ [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path]. The geographical layer includes regional influences [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]; for the founder, the region is the Bengal delta, with its rivers, monsoon and settlement patterns.

### Global

‘The space's universal values’ [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path]: universal knowledge regarding ‘structural principles, material science, and technological innovation’, embraced but critically adapted to the local [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. The approach describes itself as ‘locally rooted, globally aware, and future-ready’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

## Contextual Analysis and Interpretation

Reading comes first: ‘Before form or function, it is necessary to understand the context’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. The analysis looks backwards in order to look forwards. It ‘examines a context's past and developmental trends. By following these trends, it identifies future needs and aspirations to guide forward-thinking design’ [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path]. Understanding these trends, the founder writes, ‘allows for an accurate projection of future aspirations, which often differs from general perceptions’ [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path].
