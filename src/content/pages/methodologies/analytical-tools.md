The published texts do not prescribe a set of analytical tools. The kinds of analysis described below are those the published case studies and student projects report using, together with the editorial tools offered on this platform. Each is marked as such.

## Context Mapping and Documentation

Mapping appears in the case studies as a way of recording what the reading finds. The Panatirtha thesis ‘mapped memory routes connecting temple and settlement’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. A 2023 thesis presented as founded on New Contextualism reports that ‘a comprehensive site analysis was undertaken, examining various dimensions including the urban, economic, cultural, social, and geographical aspects’ [@arch-bangla-2024-ahsania-mission-children-city].

The founder’s earlier practice includes the documentation of traditional settlements in Oman for heritage management plans [@bandyopadhyay-et-al-2014-bahla; @bracu-faculty-profile]. For recording a reading layer by layer, this platform offers an editorial [ten-layer reading sheet](/methodologies/tools-and-resources/).

## Stakeholder and Social Analysis

The social layer reads community structures, demographics and lifestyles [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. In the Panatirtha thesis, ‘social & cultural layers revealed how pilgrimage and tourism could sustain the economy of the permanent community’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. The 2026 article on Somapura Mahavihara emphasises ‘inclusive stakeholder engagement’ [@reza-rahman-2026-somapura].

## Historical and Spatial Analysis

Historical analysis is fundamental to the approach: ‘A deep, critical engagement with history is not an optional academic pursuit for the architect; it is fundamental’ [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. It examines ‘a context's past and developmental trends’ in order to identify future needs [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path]. In the case studies it is joined to spatial analysis: ‘Historical & spatial layers traced community paths across paddy fields’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

## Systems and Relationship Diagrams

The approach is described as ‘incorporating systems thinking’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice], but the published texts do not include diagrams of systems or relationships. The diagrams on this platform are editorial: the [contextual diagram and relations matrix](/methodologies/foundational-framework/), and the ten marks that show which layers a project foregrounds. They can be downloaded from [Tools and Resources](/methodologies/tools-and-resources/).

## Participatory Assessment

The approach is described as ‘incorporating systems thinking, regenerative ethics, and participatory design’, and its example of a heritage eco-resort ‘could apply community co-creation, unlike typical Critical Regionalism, which rarely includes participatory economic planning’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. The 2026 article on Somapura Mahavihara gives a place to ‘community participation’ [@reza-rahman-2026-somapura].
