If context is not limited to the site, where does it begin and end? The founder’s 2023 essay describes a sequence of scales: context ‘starts from the space’s universal values, extends to regional ideas aligned with critical regionalism, and encompasses the immediate neighbourhood and site boundaries’, with each examined through its past and its developmental trends [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path].

This places New Contextualism in conversation with critical regionalism, which sought an architecture capable of mediating between universal civilisation and the particularities of place [@frampton-1983-critical-regionalism]. The founder locates the approach within the discourse of contextualism and critical regionalism, naming Robert Venturi, Charles Jencks and Kenneth Frampton, while placing ‘greater emphasis on sustainability and community-oriented design’, as championed by Christopher Alexander, Jane Jacobs, Jan Gehl and Bjarke Ingels [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path].

The relationship between the universal and the particular is described as hybridisation. New Contextualism ‘embraces this global knowledge but insists on its critical adaptation to the specificities of the local’, rejecting the wholesale importation of ‘International Styles’ as firmly as parochial isolationism [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. Heritage, in this view, is ‘not a static relic to be cordoned off or merely replicated’ but ‘a vital, living continuum’ that should actively inform contemporary design: ‘building upon legacy, not just beside it’ [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh].
