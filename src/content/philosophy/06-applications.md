In practice, New Contextualism is presented as a process before it is a form. Every project is said to begin with three phases, Read, Plan and Process, with particular weight given to the first: ‘Before form or function, it is necessary to understand the context’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. The ten dimensions give that reading its structure.

The published record of applications consists mainly of academic design proposals: undergraduate theses, studio projects and competition entries. They include a thesis that reimagines a pilgrimage site at Panatirtha, Sunamganj, as a civic landscape, and another that frames Dhaka’s neglected Kallyanpur canal as a civic spine within a transit-oriented development [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]; a proposal for the regeneration of Old Dhaka’s Chawk Bazar through phased intervention and adaptive reuse; a conceptual proposal for an Islamic fortress complex in Spain; and a community-based tourism proposal for a village known for its Alpana art [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. These are conceptual proposals, not built works. Each is documented in the Projects section with its sources.

The April 2025 essay extends the approach through four areas of emphasis: nature-inspired design, deep sustainability integration, alignment with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, and proactive resilience [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh; @united-nations-2015-agenda-2030].
