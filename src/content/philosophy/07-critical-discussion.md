As a philosophy still in development, New Contextualism invites critical examination, and its own public events have made room for it. The 2023 workshop included a lecture on the possibilities and challenges of the approach, which ‘critically analysed the ten key parameters’ and identified those with more scope for development [@bracu-2023-workshop-report; @bhuiyan-2023-possibilities-challenges]. The 2024 workshop programme likewise announced discussion of ‘New Contextualism’s potential and limitations’ [@contextbd-2024-workshop-listing]. The questions below are offered in that spirit, as editorial commentary rather than settled positions.

**Weighting the dimensions.** The published texts state that not every layer carries equal weight in each project, but they do not yet set out how weights are determined, or by whom. A transparent method for weighting dimensions, and for resolving conflicts between them, would strengthen the framework’s claim to be replicable.

**Prediction and its limits.** The approach places considerable trust in prediction, including big data and AI. Its texts qualify this: such tools are to be used responsibly [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice], and their accuracy depends on an understanding of historical precedent [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. Questions of data access, bias and accountability, particularly in the Global South, remain open.

**Stability of the framework.** The dimensions and principles have been formulated in slightly different ways across publications (see section 04). A definitive, citable statement approved by the originator would help others apply and critique the framework consistently.

**Evidence from practice.** The documented applications are, so far, academic and conceptual proposals. Built work, post-occupancy evaluation and comparative studies would allow the approach’s claims about equity, resilience and belonging to be tested.

**Relation to earlier theory.** New Contextualism presents itself as process-based rather than style-based, drawing on Modernism, Postmodernism and critical regionalism ‘in a more nurtured and advanced manner’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. Careful comparison with that literature is a promising line of research. One example is participatory economic planning, which the September 2025 essay suggests critical regionalism rarely includes.

**Future directions.** The founder’s writing anticipates further development through New Contextualism 2.0 [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh], and the 2023 essay was presented as a summary of a forthcoming article for a book dedicated to the philosophy [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path].
