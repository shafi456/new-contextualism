New Contextualism is an approach to architecture and urban design originated by Mohammad Habib Reza, an architect, architectural historian and Associate Professor at the School of Architecture and Design, BRAC University, Dhaka [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. Reports of its first public workshop describe it as a philosophy ‘coined by Mohammad Habib Reza’ that took shape at BRAC University over the course of a decade [@bracu-2023-workshop-report; @arch-bangla-2023-workshop-report].

In the founder’s own account, the approach grew from two sources. The first was practice. As an architect-planner for the Barisal City Corporation, working on area development projects such as the Bibir Pukur region and the Multipurpose City Market, he was confronted with the need to reconcile new interventions with existing urban fabric in a deltaic environment [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path; @reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. The second was research. His doctoral study of the early Buddhist architecture of Bengal, at sites such as Mahasthangarh and Wari-Bateshwar, revealed buildings shaped by climate, local materials, ritual and social organisation [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh].

The approach has since been developed through teaching. The founder describes encouraging students to undertake ‘a tenfold overlapping study of the broader context’ in his design studios [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path], and his 2025 biography refers to numerous undergraduate thesis studios on the philosophy at BRAC University [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. Its public articulation can be traced through workshops, essays and symposia from 2023 onwards, set out in the chronology below. The 2025 essay in *The Daily Star* names a further stage, ‘New Contextualism 2.0’, with a more explicit integration of sustainability frameworks [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh].
