The published announcement describes a workshop combining lectures, open to all, with hands-on exercises and discussions on: the principles of New Contextualism; the impact of context on design decisions; case studies of New Contextual projects; global practices adopting the approach; possibilities and challenges in contemporary design; and the approach’s potential and limitations [@contextbd-2024-workshop-listing].

Ten alumni of the Department of Architecture were announced as mentors for the hands-on exercises [@contextbd-2024-workshop-listing].

A report published after the workshop records an introductory session by Mohammad Habib Reza, followed by lectures. Tanjina Khan spoke on how contemporary architecture and urban design integrate New Contextual principles, and S M Kaikobad presented case studies of New Contextual projects worldwide. Mohammad Zillur Rahman set out the methodology of applying the approach to architectural and urban projects, drawing on earlier student work. Md Samiur Rahman Bhuiyan addressed its challenges and prospects, suggesting that it ‘could be termed SDG+ (Sustainable Development Goal Plus)’ [@arch-bangla-2024-workshop-report].
