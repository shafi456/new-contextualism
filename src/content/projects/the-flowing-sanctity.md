context: >-
  Panatirtha is a spiritual pilgrimage site in Sunamganj, Bangladesh, where Advaita Acharya’s temple stands. Traditionally visited only once a year for ritual bathing, the site risked slipping into obscurity [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].
historical: >-
  Historical and spatial readings traced community paths across paddy fields and mapped memory routes connecting temple and settlement [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].
social: >-
  Social and cultural readings revealed how pilgrimage and tourism could sustain the economy of the permanent community; design interventions therefore sought to strengthen both local livelihood and artistic vitality [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].
cultural: >-
  Spiritual and cultural concepts, especially Advaita, or non-dualism, shaped the spatial form [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].
process: >-
  The essay presents the thesis as a project in which ‘context was not decorative but rather was the design generator’ [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. The routes traced in the historical and spatial reading became the skeleton of new circulation networks, so that the space would be grounded in familiar movement rather than feeling alien [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].
response: >-
  The design proposes memory lanes, public spaces, green buffers and cultural nodes that transform the river edge into a civic landscape. Courts for culture and for ritual are interwoven rather than divided, open to all identities, and year-round cultural festivals, community hubs and local markets are proposed to activate the spiritual complex [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].
