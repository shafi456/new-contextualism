Mohammad Habib Reza is an architect and architectural historian, and an Associate Professor at the School of Architecture and Design, BRAC University, Dhaka, specialising in architectural and urban design history and theory and in heritage management [@reza-2025-deltaic-bangladesh]. He completed a Bachelor of Architecture at Khulna University in 2001 and a PhD in architectural history and theory at Nottingham Trent University in 2013 [@bracu-faculty-profile].

Before his academic career he worked as an architect for the Barisal City Corporation [@bracu-faculty-profile], the experience he identifies as the point at which he first felt the need for a contextual approach [@reza-2023-new-contextualism-path]. At Nottingham Trent University he was a Research Fellow on projects funded by the university and Oman’s Ministry of Heritage and Culture, documenting traditional oasis settlements in central Oman for heritage management plans [@bracu-faculty-profile]. He is a founding member of ArCHIAM, the Centre for the Study of Architecture and Cultural Heritage in India, Arabia and the Maghreb [@bracu-faculty-profile; @reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice].

His research addresses the geometric and morphological development of architecture and settlements and their connection with cultural, social and religious processes, with a particular focus on the ancient and medieval Buddhist architecture of Bengal [@bracu-faculty-profile]. Over the past decade he has developed New Contextualism through teaching, writing, workshops and symposia [@reza-nitu-2025-people-place-justice]. His recent essays address the cultural and civilisational history of Bengal, and a 2026 author note describes his work on ‘Bengal’s cultural history, deltaic urbanism, heritage governance, and civilisational continuity’ [@reza-2026-bongabdo; @reza-2026-reading-bengal].
