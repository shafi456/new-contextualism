# **WEBSITE DESIGN PROMPT — NEW CONTEXTUALISM**

## **1\. Project Overview**

Design and develop a sophisticated, minimalist, philosophy-driven website for **New Contextualism**, an architectural and urban design movement originated by Mohammad Habib Reza.

The website must function as the movement's official digital platform, intellectual archive, and international academic presence. It should communicate the movement's philosophical foundations, document its research and projects, and invite architects, urban designers, academics, researchers, and students to engage with its ideas.

This is NOT an architecture firm's portfolio website, a commercial design agency, or a generic institutional website.

It should feel like a combination of:

* An independent philosophical journal.  
* An international architectural research platform.  
* A contemporary intellectual movement.  
* A carefully curated archive of architectural thought.  
* A minimalist editorial publication.

The website must communicate intellectual depth, clarity, restraint, and critical thinking through its design.

## **2\. Core Concept**

**Working title:** NEW CONTEXTUALISM

**Proposed tagline:** Understanding Context. Reimagining Futures.

Treat this tagline as draft branding until approved by the movement's founder.

The central concept is that architecture cannot be understood through physical form alone. It emerges from relationships among society, history, culture, economics, politics, ecology, geography, technology, urban systems, and architectural conditions.

The website should communicate this philosophy through its content, typography, spatial composition, visual language, navigation, and interaction design.

The interface itself should demonstrate contextual thinking: every element should relate meaningfully to the whole.

Avoid decorative elements without intellectual or functional purpose.

## **3\. Visual Identity**

Create a distinctive editorial design system inspired by contemporary architecture journals, philosophical publications, modernist graphic design, and independent research platforms.

### **Color palette**

Use a restrained, predominantly monochromatic palette:

* Warm paper white: \#F1EFE9  
* Deep charcoal: \#24241F  
* Soft grey: \#88877F  
* Fine divider grey: \#D4D1C8  
* Muted brick red: \#A54B37, used sparingly  
* Desaturated sage: \#B7B9A6, used selectively in diagrams

The interface should remain approximately 85–90% neutral in appearance.

Do not use gradients, neon colors, glossy effects, excessive shadows, colorful cards, or trendy startup aesthetics.

### **Typography**

Use an editorial typographic hierarchy.

* Headlines: an elegant serif such as Instrument Serif, Cormorant Garamond, or an equivalent.  
* Body text: Inter, Manrope, or a comparable highly legible sans serif.  
* Metadata: compact uppercase sans-serif labels with restrained letter spacing.

Use oversized typography selectively, with generous whitespace and deliberate line breaks.

Typography should feel intellectual and architectural rather than luxurious or corporate.

### **Layout principles**

* Wide margins and generous negative space.  
* A consistent 12-column desktop grid.  
* Asymmetrical editorial compositions.  
* Thin rules and subtle dividers.  
* Large, carefully cropped architectural photographs.  
* Small uppercase section identifiers.  
* Minimal navigation and understated transitions.  
* Strong alignment and precise spacing.  
* Responsive layouts designed for desktop, tablet, and mobile.

The website should feel quiet, contemplative, precise, and confident.

## **4\. Homepage Design**

Create a complete homepage with the following sections.

### **A. Minimal navigation**

Design a clean horizontal navigation bar.

Left:  
NEW CONTEXTUALISM

Right:

* The Movement  
* Philosophy  
* Framework  
* Projects  
* Research  
* About

Include a minimal search icon and a compact mobile menu.

Keep the navigation visually light, with no oversized buttons or distracting background treatments.

The header may become sticky on scroll, provided the behavior remains subtle.

### **B. Hero section**

Use an expansive, minimalist hero section.

Display a large typographic heading:

NEW  
CONTEXTUALISM

Below it:

Understanding Context. Reimagining Futures.

Add a short introduction:

"An architectural and urban design philosophy exploring the relationships between people, place, history, culture, ecology, and the built environment."

Include two understated text links:

Explore the Philosophy ↗

Discover the Research ↗

Use a large, atmospheric architectural photograph or an original abstract architectural composition. Favor spatial depth, materiality, shadow, landscape, urban patterns, and subtle human presence.

Avoid generic skyscraper photographs, corporate stock photography, and futuristic CGI.

The hero should feel like the opening page of a philosophical publication.

### **C. Philosophical introduction**

Section label:  
01 / THE IDEA

Headline:  
"Context is more than what surrounds us."

Body text:

"Every place is shaped by visible and invisible forces. Histories, social relationships, cultural practices, ecological processes, political structures, and everyday life contribute to the meaning of the built environment.

New Contextualism approaches architecture and urbanism through these interconnected conditions, seeking a deeper understanding of place and the possibilities for meaningful transformation."

Present the text with generous whitespace. Consider an asymmetric layout with the heading occupying one-third of the grid and the paragraph occupying the remaining space.

### **D. The philosophical framework**

Section label:  
02 / THE FRAMEWORK

Headline:  
"Ten dimensions. Interconnected realities."

Introduce the ten contextual dimensions:

01 — Social  
02 — Economic  
03 — Political  
04 — Historical  
05 — Ecological  
06 — Scientific and Technological  
07 — Geographical  
08 — Cultural  
09 — Urban  
10 — Architectural

Present these as a refined typographic grid, not colorful cards.

Each dimension should be clickable and lead to a dedicated page containing:

* A clear definition.  
* Key research questions.  
* Analytical diagrams.  
* Relevant publications.  
* Related projects.  
* Connections to other dimensions.

Include a subtle interactive feature that allows visitors to explore relationships between the dimensions.

Do not present the ten dimensions as completely independent categories. Show how they interact.

Verify the final framework against the founder's approved publications before publishing.

### **E. Featured research and projects**

Section label:  
03 / IN PRACTICE

Headline:  
"Thinking through places."

Create an editorial project gallery with large images, carefully typeset titles, locations, publication dates, and brief descriptions.

Each project should have its own page documenting:

* Context and background.  
* Research questions.  
* Historical, social, cultural, and ecological conditions.  
* Analytical process.  
* Design response.  
* Outcomes and critical reflections.  
* Contributors and references.

Use asymmetrical image layouts and restrained typography.

Do not invent projects, results, awards, or affiliations. Populate the gallery only with verified materials.

Clearly distinguish completed projects, ongoing research, and conceptual proposals.

### **F. Founder and intellectual origins**

Section label:  
04 / THE ORIGIN

Headline:  
"An evolving architectural philosophy."

Introduce Mohammad Habib Reza as the originator of New Contextualism, using verified biographical information and approved institutional affiliations.

Include:

* A portrait or approved photograph.  
* A short biography.  
* The intellectual origins of the philosophy.  
* Selected publications and lectures.  
* Links to relevant research.

Do not fabricate quotations or attribute draft statements to the founder.

Provide a link:  
Explore the Movement's Origins ↗

### **G. Research and publications**

Section label:  
05 / THE ARCHIVE

Headline:  
"An ongoing conversation."

Create a publication-led section with a minimalist list of selected books, academic papers, essays, interviews, lectures, and conference contributions.

Each record should include the title, author, year, publication type, and source link.

Provide filtering by:

* Publication type.  
* Author.  
* Year.  
* Contextual dimension.  
* Topic.

Use a scholarly editorial presentation rather than a conventional blog-card grid.

### **H. International dialogue**

Section label:  
06 / THE COMMUNITY

Headline:  
"Context crosses disciplines."

Introduce the platform as a space for international academic dialogue.

Include understated links for:

* Research contributions.  
* Academic collaborations.  
* Lectures and seminars.  
* Calls for papers.  
* Workshops and events.

Any submission or membership feature must be clearly defined and genuinely functional. Do not imply an established membership organization if none has been formally created.

### **I. Footer**

Use a simple, spacious footer.

Include:  
NEW CONTEXTUALISM

Architecture. Context. Relationships.

Navigation:  
Movement / Philosophy / Framework / Projects / Research / About

Additional links:

Include verified institutional links and social media accounts only when available.

## **5\. Philosophy Page**

Develop a dedicated page explaining the movement in depth.

Suggested structure:

1. Origins and historical development.  
2. The limitations of understanding context through physical form alone.  
3. The relationship between architecture and its broader environment.  
4. The ten contextual dimensions.  
5. Context as a dynamic and interconnected system.  
6. Applications in architecture and urban design.  
7. Critical discussion, limitations, and future directions.  
8. Selected references and further reading.

Use large editorial headings, short paragraphs, diagrams, quotations only from verified sources, and a visible bibliography.

Do not present New Contextualism as a universally standardized theory with a single uncontested definition. Explain its intellectual development through attributable sources.

## **6\. Interactive Contextual Diagram**

Create a distinctive interactive visualization of the ten dimensions.

Display the dimensions as interconnected nodes or a layered spatial diagram.

When a visitor selects a dimension:

* Highlight the selected dimension.  
* Reveal its definition.  
* Display related questions.  
* Show its connections with other dimensions.  
* Link to relevant research and projects.

Use subtle animations and thin connecting lines.

Avoid dashboard aesthetics, excessive colors, complex 3D effects, or gamification.

The visualization should resemble a carefully designed architectural research diagram.

It must be functional, accessible, responsive, and usable with a keyboard.

## **7\. Image Direction**

Use visual material that supports the intellectual character of the movement.

Preferred imagery:

* Architectural photography with strong composition and material depth.  
* Historic and contemporary urban environments.  
* Landscape and ecological systems.  
* Urban morphology and mapping.  
* Architectural plans, sections, and analytical diagrams.  
* Archival documents and annotated drawings.  
* People interacting with their everyday environments.

Prioritize actual projects and verified documentary material.

Do not generate fabricated historical photographs or misrepresent conceptual images as completed architecture.

Give every image appropriate credit and alternative text.

## **8\. Technical Requirements**

Build a real, functional, responsive website rather than a static visual mockup.

Recommended architecture:

* Next.js or Astro for the frontend.  
* A headless CMS such as Sanity or Strapi for editorial management.  
* Structured content models for publications, authors, projects, events, and contextual dimensions.  
* Searchable research archive.  
* Reusable editorial page templates.  
* Accessible navigation and interaction components.  
* Responsive image optimization.  
* Search-engine metadata and semantic HTML.  
* Persistent, shareable URLs for publications and projects.

Provide an editorial interface that allows authorized editors to add, revise, and archive content without editing source code.

Use a maintainable design system and reusable components.

Do not build unnecessary features that compromise simplicity or reliability.

## **9\. Motion and Interaction**

Use motion sparingly.

Acceptable interactions:

* Subtle opacity transitions.  
* Gentle image reveals.  
* Understated link hover states.  
* Minimal menu transitions.  
* Carefully controlled scrolling behavior.  
* Smooth highlighting in the contextual diagram.

Avoid:

* Scroll hijacking.  
* Excessive parallax.  
* Cursor-following effects.  
* Unnecessary loading animations.  
* Dramatic page transitions.  
* Motion that distracts from reading.

Respect reduced-motion accessibility preferences.

## **10\. Content Integrity and Editorial Governance**

The website represents a real philosophical movement and its originator.

Clearly distinguish:

* Verified statements from published sources.  
* Editorial summaries.  
* Proposed mission and vision statements.  
* Founder-approved quotations.  
* Interpretations and critical commentary.

Do not fabricate official manifestos, endorsements, affiliations, biographies, publications, or quotations.

Require approval for the final public-facing philosophy, founder profile, and official branding.

Use proper academic citations and provide source links wherever possible.

## **11\. Final Design Standard**

The final website should feel like a serious international platform for architectural philosophy.

It should be:

* Minimal, not empty.  
* Philosophical, not abstract to the point of obscurity.  
* Academic, not bureaucratic.  
* Contemporary, not trendy.  
* Visually sophisticated, not decorative.  
* Research-driven, not promotional.  
* International in scope, while remaining attentive to local contexts.

The design should express the core idea through its own composition: relationships between text and image, structure and space, research and practice, and local specificity and wider systems.

**Final instruction:** Build the complete homepage and all primary navigation pages with a coherent visual identity, realistic content structure, functional links, responsive behavior, and a working interactive contextual framework. Prioritize intellectual clarity, editorial restraint, and long-term credibility over visual effects.

The result should look and feel like the digital home of an evolving architectural and philosophical movement.
